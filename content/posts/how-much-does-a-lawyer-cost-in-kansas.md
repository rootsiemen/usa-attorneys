Kansas attorneys charge some of the most reasonable legal rates in the country — and almost nobody in Kansas knows what those rates are supposed to be, because Kansas law firms advertise services, not prices. So here is the statewide picture for 2026. A lawyer in Kansas typically charges **$150 to $350 per hour**, with Wichita, Topeka, and Lawrence clustered at the lower half and the Kansas City suburbs (Overland Park, Olathe, Lenexa) at the top. Flat fees carry most routine work: a first-offense DUI defense commonly runs $2,000 to $6,000, an uncontested divorce $1,200 to $2,500 plus filing fees, a simple will $250 to $750, and an LLC formation $500 to $1,200 plus the state filing fee.

Kansas also has two rules that change legal math in ways most residents never learn until they are inside a case: a hard statutory cap on workers compensation attorney fees, and a $4,000 ceiling on small claims that routes thousands of disputes away from lawyers entirely. Both are covered below, with the numbers worked out.

## What Kansas attorneys charge by practice area

- **DUI and traffic defense:** Kansas first-offense [DUI representation](/dui-attorney) typically prices at $2,000 to $6,000 as a flat or staged fee, depending on whether the administrative license hearing and a jury trial are in play. Second and third offenses move to $5,000 to $12,000. Compare the national picture in our [DUI attorney cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-dui-attorney-cost-2026) — Kansas sits modestly below the national midpoint, but the collateral costs (fines, ignition interlock, insurance) exceed the legal fee in most first-offense cases, which is why the quality of the defense matters more than a $500 discount.
- **Divorce and family law:** $175 to $325 per hour is the working band; uncontested cases with a full agreement flat-fee at $1,200 to $2,500. Contested custody or farm-asset divorces run $6,000 to $20,000. Kansas is an equitable distribution state, and in agricultural counties the valuation of land, equipment, and crop income — not the hourly rate — is what decides the final bill.
- **Personal injury:** contingency at the customary one-third of the recovery (33⅓%), with no upfront fee in a standard engagement. Kansas follows modified comparative fault with a 51% bar: you recover only if you are less at fault than the party you are suing.
- **Estate planning and probate:** wills $250 to $750, full estate packages with powers of attorney and health care directives $600 to $1,500, trust packages $1,500 to $3,000. Smaller Kansas estates may qualify for simplified procedures, which is one reason probate here tends to cost less than in statutory-fee states.
- **Business and farm law:** $200 to $350 per hour in the KC metro, $150 to $275 elsewhere; entity formation, operating agreements, and farm succession documents are the bread-and-butter flat-fee items.
- **Real estate:** Kansas does not require an attorney at closing. Sellers and buyers who hire one anyway pay $500 to $1,200 for a residential transaction package — and FSBO sellers in particular should read the [for-sale-by-owner attorney fee math](/blog/real-estate-attorney-fees-for-sale-by-owner) before deciding to skip it.

## The Kansas workers comp rule: attorney fees capped at 25% by law

Kansas is one of the states where the legislature, not the market, sets the ceiling for one entire category of legal fees. Under K.S.A. 44-536, an attorney's fee in a Kansas workers compensation case may not exceed **25% of the compensation awarded** — and fees are subject to approval, which means the cap is enforced, not aspirational.

Run the arithmetic on a typical award. A $48,000 workers comp settlement or award in Kansas produces a maximum attorney fee of **$12,000**. The same injury settled as a general personal injury claim at a one-third contingency would carry a $16,000 fee. The statute saves the worker $4,000 on that award — and it also changes lawyer behavior: Kansas [workers compensation attorneys](/workers-compensation-attorney) are selective about small or doubtful claims because the capped fee on a $9,000 award is $2,250 for what may be months of work. If your comp claim was denied, that selectivity is not a verdict on your case; it is arithmetic. Bring organized medical records and the employer's First Report of Injury to the first meeting, because a claim that arrives pre-documented is a claim a capped-fee lawyer can afford to take. Our [workers comp attorney cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-workers-comp-attorney-cost-2026) works through the national comparison, and the [settlement payout math](/blog/workers-compensation-settlement-payout-math) shows what awards themselves look like before fees.

## Wichita and Topeka vs. the Kansas City suburbs: the two-market state

Kansas legal pricing splits along one line: the Kansas City metropolitan area versus everywhere else. In **Johnson County and the KC suburbs**, where firms compete for Missouri-side clients and corporate work, experienced counsel runs $250 to $350 an hour and family-law retainers commonly start at $3,500 to $5,000. In **Wichita**, the state's largest true Kansas market, the same experience band is $200 to $300. In **Topeka, Lawrence, Manhattan, and the regional hubs** (Dodge City, Garden City, Hays, Salina), $150 to $250 covers most practice areas — and agricultural law expertise there is deeper than anything the metro offers at any price.

Two practical consequences. First, document work travels: a Sedgwick County business owner forming an LLC or a Shawnee County couple writing wills can hire Salina or Manhattan counsel at a 20% to 30% discount without losing anything, because the work product is identical. Second, litigation does not travel: a case in Johnson County District Court wants a lawyer who appears there weekly, and Kansas has 31 judicial districts with genuinely local habits. Pay the metro rate when the courtroom is in the metro; save it when the work is paper.

## Kansas court quirks that change your bill before you hire anyone

- **Small claims ceiling of $4,000.** Kansas small claims court handles claims up to $4,000, and lawyers are generally not required — the procedure is designed for self-representation, with filing fees that are a rounding error next to an attorney's retainer. Between $4,000 and roughly the cost of two billable hours, a paid consultation plus a lawyer-drafted demand letter is usually the economically correct product.
- **No-fault auto insurance (PIP).** Kansas requires personal injury protection coverage with at least $4,500 in medical benefits, paid regardless of fault. After a crash, the first dollars of medical bills route through your own PIP — which is why a Kansas [personal injury consultation](/personal-injury-lawyer) starts with your policy declarations page, not the other driver's insurer.
- **No attorney required at real estate closings.** Closings here are run by title companies and lenders. Optional does not mean useless — see the FSBO analysis linked above — but nobody in Kansas is legally forced to buy closing counsel the way buyers in attorney-closing states are.
- **Comparative fault at 51%.** Kansas bars recovery if you are 51% or more at fault. Fee-wise, this is why Kansas injury lawyers investigate fault allocation before quoting anything beyond the standard contingency.

## The retainer question: what Kansas firms ask for upfront, and what is refundable

Ask a Wichita family lawyer to start work and you will hear a number between $1,500 and $5,000 called a retainer. Ask a Kansas City firm and the same word may mean something structurally different, so pin it down. In Kansas practice the word covers three products. A **general (true) retainer** buys the lawyer's availability for a period and may be earned on receipt if the agreement says so clearly. An **advance fee deposit** — the common model — is your money held in the lawyer's trust account and drawn down as work is billed; unearned portions must be refunded when the engagement ends, because Kansas ethics rules treat advance fees as client property until earned. An **evergreen retainer** requires you to top the deposit back up to the agreed floor whenever it dips, which is how a $2,500 retainer quietly becomes $9,000 across a long custody case. Before signing, get three lines into the agreement: the replenishment trigger, the billing increment (six-minute tenths are standard; fifteen-minute minimums are a quiet tax), and the refund mechanics at termination. Kansas clients who skip these questions are not overpaying their lawyer's rate — they are overpaying their lawyer's structure, which is the more expensive mistake.

## How to hire Kansas counsel without overpaying

Start with the [Kansas attorney directory](/attorneys/kansas) and shortlist by practice area and city, then run three quotes — one local, one from the nearest metro, one from a regional hub — because the spread inside Kansas is wide enough to matter. In the first meeting, ask the four questions that expose the real price: Is this flat, hourly, or hybrid? What retainer is required, and is any of it refundable if we settle early (the rules in our [retainer agreement guide](/blog/retainer-agreement-clauses-to-read-before-signing) apply verbatim in Kansas)? Who besides you will bill my file? And what does this cost if it goes the long way, not the short way?

If your matter touches another state — a custody case split with [Maryland](/attorneys/maryland), a business with operations in [New Mexico](/attorneys/new-mexico) — ask whether the Kansas firm will coordinate with out-of-state counsel or hand you off entirely, because coordination time is billed time.

The through-line of every number in this guide: Kansas attorneys are not cheap because the work is worth less. They are cheaper because Kansas overhead is lower and two of its fee rules — the 25% comp cap and the $4,000 small claims ceiling — are written to keep ordinary people out of expensive legal process. Hire with the table above in front of you, and the first quote you get will rarely be the one you should take.
