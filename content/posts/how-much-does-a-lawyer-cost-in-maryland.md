Type "attorney maryland" into a search engine and you get what every directory gives you: names, headshots, star ratings — everything except the one thing you actually need, which is a number. So here is the number, up front. A lawyer in Maryland typically charges **$175 to $550 per hour** in 2026, with the spread driven less by the lawyer's talent than by two things: what part of Maryland you are standing in, and what kind of trouble you are in. Flat fees cover most routine work: an uncontested divorce runs $1,000 to $2,500 plus filing fees, a first-offense DUI defense $2,000 to $5,000, a simple will $300 to $1,000, and a residential real estate settlement $750 to $1,500 when you hire your own attorney.

Maryland also has one legal rule that quietly reprices every injury case in the state — contributory negligence — and we will get to it, because it explains why Maryland injury lawyers screen cases the way they do and why the fee conversation here is different from almost every other state. First, the table the directories never show you.

## The 2026 Maryland fee table, practice area by practice area

These are the working ranges Maryland clients actually paid in 2026, gathered the way fees really work — by billing model, not by advertising slogan:

- **Personal injury:** contingency, almost always 33⅓% of the recovery if the case settles before suit, rising to 40% once a complaint is filed. No upfront cost in a standard engagement. (The full math of how that percentage gets calculated — before or after costs — is in our [personal injury contingency fee breakdown](/blog/personal-injury-lawyer-contingency-fee-math).)
- **Divorce and family law:** $250 to $450 per hour in the Baltimore metro and DC suburbs; uncontested cases with a signed separation agreement often flat-fee at $1,000 to $2,500. Contested divorces with custody or business assets run $7,500 to $25,000 and beyond. For the national benchmark, compare our [divorce attorney cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-divorce-attorney-cost-2026).
- **DUI and criminal defense:** first-offense DUI $2,000 to $5,000 flat in most counties; misdemeanor defense $1,500 to $4,000; felony representation is usually hourly or a five-figure flat fee staged by case phase.
- **Estate planning and probate:** a will package (will, advance directive, powers of attorney) $750 to $1,800 for an individual, $1,200 to $2,500 for a couple; a revocable trust package $1,800 to $3,500. Probate administration is partly statutory — Maryland's Register of Wills charges court fees on a schedule tied to the size of the estate, and attorney compensation for estates is subject to court approval, which disciplines fees in a way hourly states never see.
- **Business and contracts:** $275 to $500 per hour at established firms in the I-270 and Baltimore corridors; entity formation $750 to $1,500 plus the state filing fee, contract drafting $500 to $2,000 for standard commercial documents.
- **Real estate:** settlements in Maryland are run by title companies or settlement attorneys. If you bring your own lawyer to review and protect your side, expect $750 to $1,500 for a residential purchase or sale.

## Attorney Maryland shoppers should understand: contributory negligence reprices everything

Maryland is one of only four states — with Virginia, North Carolina, and Alabama, plus the District of Columbia — that still applies **pure contributory negligence** to injury cases. If you are found even 1% at fault for your own injury, you recover nothing. Not a reduced amount. Nothing.

This single doctrine explains three things about Maryland injury fees that confuse people comparing across state lines. First, Maryland [personal injury lawyers](/personal-injury-lawyer) reject cases that would be perfectly good in Pennsylvania or Ohio, because a whiff of shared fault can zero the case at trial. Second, the cases they do take get worked harder at the investigation stage — evidence is gathered early, while the fault picture can still be shaped honestly. Third, insurance adjusters in Maryland open negotiations more aggressively than anywhere else in the region, because they know the doctrine is behind them. If you are screening [attorneys in Maryland](/attorneys/maryland) for an injury case, the fee percentage matters less than whether the lawyer can explain, in your first meeting, exactly how contributory negligence applies to your facts and what the investigation plan is. A lawyer who cannot do that at any percentage is expensive.

The doctrine also has a practical exception worth knowing: the "last clear chance" rule and specific protections for children can soften it, and Maryland's legislature has repeatedly debated replacing it with comparative fault without doing so. As of 2026, the old rule stands.

## Baltimore and the DC suburbs vs. everywhere else: the rate map

Maryland is really three legal markets wearing one state bar. In **Montgomery County and the DC suburbs** (Bethesda, Rockville, Silver Spring), rates shadow Washington, DC: $350 to $550 an hour for experienced counsel, family-law and business work at the top of that band. In the **Baltimore metro** (Baltimore City, Baltimore County, Anne Arundel, Howard), expect $250 to $450, with downtown firms at the top and neighborhood practices near the bottom. On the **Eastern Shore, in Western Maryland, and in Southern Maryland's rural counties**, $175 to $300 covers most practice areas, and flat fees run 15% to 30% below the metro numbers for identical documents.

The strategic implication is underused: for document-driven work — estate plans, entity formation, uncontested matters — a Maryland client in Bethesda can often hire excellent counsel in Frederick or Annapolis at two-thirds the rate, because the work never requires a courtroom. For litigation, the calculus flips: local counsel who knows the circuit court's scheduling habits and the local bench is worth a premium. Maryland has 24 circuit court jurisdictions, and they do not run alike.

## The costs on top of the lawyer's fee

Budget for these separately, because none of them appear in the hourly rate:

- **Filing fees:** a Maryland circuit court civil or divorce filing generally runs about $150 to $185 depending on the county and case type; District Court filings are lower.
- **Small claims reality check:** Maryland District Court handles small claims up to $5,000, with a filing fee that is a fraction of a lawyer's first hour. Below that threshold, most people represent themselves or pay for a single consultation instead of full representation — a math exercise similar to the one in our [small claims vs. hiring a lawyer analysis](/blog/small-claims-court-vs-hiring-lawyer-dollar-math).
- **Real estate transfer costs:** Maryland's state transfer tax is 0.5% of the consideration (0.25% for qualifying first-time buyers), plus county transfer taxes and recordation taxes that vary by county — on a $500,000 purchase these government charges dwarf the settlement attorney's fee. To see what that settlement fee itself should run, read our breakdown of [the cost of a real estate lawyer at closing](/blog/real-estate-attorney-fees-closing-cost-math).
- **Expert and process costs:** medical experts in injury cases, custody evaluators in family cases, process servers and court reporters everywhere. Ask whether your fee agreement bills these at cost or with a markup.

## If these rates are out of reach: Maryland's low-cost routes

Maryland has a deeper low-cost legal infrastructure than most states its size, and it is underused because people assume they earn too much to qualify. **Maryland Legal Aid** handles civil matters for income-eligible residents statewide. The **Maryland Volunteer Lawyers Service** matches pro bono attorneys for cases that meet its guidelines, and it is stronger in the Baltimore metro than anywhere else. Every circuit court runs a **Family Law Self-Help Center** — staffed, free, and genuinely useful for uncontested divorce and custody paperwork — and the Maryland Courts Self-Help Center covers other civil matters by phone and chat for any Marylander, at any income. Finally, county bar association lawyer referral services (Baltimore City, Montgomery, Anne Arundel, and others) offer reduced-fee initial consultations, typically 30 minutes at a fixed modest rate, which is the right purchase when your question is "do I even have a case?" rather than "will you take my case?" None of these routes replaces retained counsel in a contested matter — but for the large middle of legal problems, they are the difference between Maryland's published rates and what a prepared resident actually pays.

## Cutting the Maryland legal bill without cutting corners

Three moves consistently work. **First, buy the consultation properly.** Maryland lawyers commonly charge $100 to $350 for an initial hour or waive it for injury and some criminal matters; arrive with documents organized and questions written, using a checklist mindset like the one in our [retainer agreement clause guide](/blog/retainer-agreement-clauses-to-read-before-signing), and one paid hour can replace three billable ones later. **Second, negotiate the billing model, not the rate.** A capped-fee or flat-fee arrangement for defined phases — negotiation first, litigation only if needed — transfers risk to the firm more effectively than a $25 hourly discount. **Third, compare across the state line inside the state:** get one quote from your home county and one from an adjacent market using the [Maryland attorney directory](/attorneys/maryland); the same exercise works if your matter sits near a border, since many Maryland lawyers also serve clients comparing [Kansas attorneys](/attorneys/kansas) and [New Mexico lawyers](/attorneys/new-mexico) for multi-state family or business matters and will benchmark their quotes honestly when they know you have options.

The bottom line for anyone searching for an attorney: Maryland gives you a mid-Atlantic price structure with one very old-fashioned negligence rule. Know your practice area's going rate from the table above, know which of the three Maryland markets you are buying in, and know whether contributory negligence touches your case. With those three facts in hand, every quote you receive becomes a number you can actually judge.
