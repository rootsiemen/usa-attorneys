Wisconsin lawyers advertise experience, results, and free consultations — everything except a price. So here is the price, up front. A lawyer in Wisconsin typically charges **$175 to $400 per hour** in 2026. Clio's billing data puts the statewide average at **$287 per hour**, well under the $362 national average, with Milwaukee and Madison at the top of the range and the Northwoods and Driftless markets at the bottom. Flat fees carry most routine work: a first-offense OWI defense commonly runs $1,500 to $3,500, an uncontested divorce $1,000 to $2,500 plus filing fees, a simple will $300 to $900, and an LLC formation $500 to $1,500 plus the state's $130 online filing fee.

Wisconsin also writes two of its fee rules into statute in ways that quietly save residents money: a hard 20% cap on workers compensation attorney fees, and a small claims system generous enough to route thousands of disputes around lawyers entirely — a ceiling that rises again on January 1, 2027. Both are worked out below, with the arithmetic shown.

- **Personal injury:** contingency at the customary one-third (33⅓%) of the recovery, usually rising toward 40% if the case goes to trial, with no upfront fee. Wisconsin follows modified comparative negligence with a 51% bar (Wis. Stat. § 895.045): you recover only if your share of the fault is not greater than the defendant's. The national mechanics of the percentage are in our [personal injury contingency fee breakdown](/blog/personal-injury-lawyer-contingency-fee-math).
- **Divorce and family law:** $200 to $375 per hour is the working band; uncontested cases with a full agreement flat-fee at $1,000 to $2,500. Contested custody or business-asset divorces run $6,000 to $25,000. Wisconsin is a **community property (marital property) state** — most assets acquired during the marriage start from a presumption of equal division, which shapes both the negotiation and the bill. Compare the national picture in our [divorce attorney cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-divorce-attorney-cost-2026).
- **OWI and criminal defense:** Wisconsin calls it OWI, and a first offense is priced unlike anywhere else in America — see the quirks section below. First-offense OWI defense typically flats at $1,500 to $3,500 covering the administrative review and court proceedings; second and third offenses move to $3,500 to $7,500. Misdemeanor defense runs $1,500 to $4,000; felonies are hourly or staged five-figure flat fees. The national benchmark is our [DUI attorney cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-dui-attorney-cost-2026).
- **Estate planning and probate:** wills $300 to $900, full packages with powers of attorney and health care directives $750 to $1,800, revocable trust packages $1,500 to $3,500. Most Wisconsin estates pass through **informal probate** administered by the county Register in Probate, and attorney fees are charged hourly or by agreement — Wisconsin has no statutory percentage schedule of the kind that inflates probate costs in states like Florida.
- **Business and contracts:** $225 to $400 per hour at established Milwaukee and Madison firms, $175 to $300 elsewhere; LLC formation $500 to $1,500 plus Wisconsin's $130 online Articles of Organization fee ($170 by paper), operating agreements $750 to $2,500, contract drafting $500 to $2,000 for standard commercial documents. - **Real estate:** Wisconsin closings are run by title companies and lenders; no attorney is required. Buyers and sellers who hire one anyway pay $500 to $1,500 for a residential transaction package — FSBO sellers should read the [for-sale-by-owner attorney fee math](/blog/real-estate-attorney-fees-for-sale-by-owner) before deciding to skip it, and everyone should see the national picture in [what a real estate lawyer costs at closing](/blog/real-estate-attorney-fees-closing-cost-math).
- **Bankruptcy:** Chapter 7 attorney fees in Wisconsin generally run $1,000 to $2,500 as a flat fee, plus the federal filing fee; Chapter 13 fees are set within the case plan and reviewed by the court.

## The Wisconsin workers comp rule: attorney fees capped at 20% by law

Wisconsin is one of the states where the legislature, not the market, sets the ceiling for an entire category of legal fees. Under Wis. Stat. § 102.26(2), a fee for enforcing or collecting a workers compensation claim may not exceed **20% of the amount in dispute that is compromised, awarded, or collected** — and the Department of Workforce Development (DWD) must approve the fee. Where the insurer has admitted liability, the amount is undisputed, and no hearing is needed, the cap collapses to **10%, and no more than $250**.

Run the arithmetic on a typical disputed claim. A $40,000 workers comp compromise in Wisconsin produces a maximum attorney fee of **$8,000**. The same $40,000 recovered as a general personal injury claim at a one-third contingency would carry a $13,333 fee. The statute saves the worker $5,333 on that claim — and it changes lawyer behavior the same way Kansas's 25% cap does: Wisconsin [workers compensation attorneys](/workers-compensation-attorney) are selective about small or doubtful claims, because the capped fee on a $10,000 disputed recovery is $2,000 for what may be months of work. If nothing is recovered, DWD's rules mean you owe no fee for the lawyer's time (case costs, like medical record charges, are separate). If your claim was denied outright, the appeal path and its payout math are in our guide to [what to do when a workers comp claim is denied](/blog/workers-comp-claim-denied-appeal-timeline-payout-math-2026), and the national fee comparison is in the [workers comp attorney cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-workers-comp-attorney-cost-2026).

## Milwaukee and Madison vs. everywhere else: the two-speed state

Wisconsin legal pricing splits into three markets. In **Milwaukee and its suburbs** (Waukesha, Brookfield, Wauwatosa), the state's deepest bench of firms prices experienced counsel at $275 to $450 an hour, with downtown litigation and business partners above that. In **Madison**, state-government, university, and tech work keeps rates close behind: $250 to $400 for experienced counsel. In the **regional hubs — Green Bay, Appleton, Eau Claire, La Crosse, Wausau, Racine, Kenosha** — $200 to $325 covers most practice areas, and in **smaller cities and rural counties**, $150 to $275 is the honest band for the same credentials.

Two practical consequences. First, document work travels: a Fox Valley business forming an LLC or a Superior couple writing wills can hire counsel one market down at a 20% to 30% discount, because the work product is identical and never sees a courtroom. Second, litigation does not travel: Wisconsin has 72 counties and ten judicial administrative districts, and a case in Milwaukee County Circuit Court wants a lawyer who appears there weekly. Pay the metro rate when the courtroom is in the metro; save it when the work is paper.

## Wisconsin court quirks that change your bill before you hire anyone

- **Small claims up to $10,000 — and $15,000 from January 1, 2027.** Wisconsin small claims court (Wis. Stat. ch. 799) handles money claims up to $10,000 and evictions regardless of the amount in dispute; personal injury and other tort claims are capped at $5,000. Under 2025 Wisconsin Act 105, the money limit rises to **$15,000 for cases filed on or after January 1, 2027**. Lawyers are not required, filing fees are modest, and for claims in this band a one-hour consultation plus a lawyer-drafted demand letter is usually the economically correct purchase — the same analysis we run nationally in [small claims court vs. hiring a lawyer](/blog/small-claims-court-vs-hiring-lawyer-dollar-math).
- **A first-offense OWI is not a crime in Wisconsin.** Wisconsin is the only state that treats a standard first-offense OWI as a **civil forfeiture** — $150 to $300 under Wis. Stat. § 346.65(2)(am)1, plus a mandatory $535 driver improvement surcharge, court costs, and a 6-to-9-month license revocation. No jail, no criminal record. It becomes criminal if a child under 16 was in the vehicle or someone was injured. The catch: it still counts as a prior for any future offense, and it never really leaves your driving record — which is why $1,500 to $3,500 of defense on a "traffic ticket" is often money well spent.
- **Community property changes divorce math.** Wisconsin's marital property law presumes an equal split of marital assets, so the expensive fights concentrate on classification (what is individual vs. marital property), business and farm valuations, and maintenance — not on the hourly rate.
- **No attorney required at real estate closings.** Title companies run Wisconsin closings. Optional does not mean useless — contract review is where FSBO and first-time buyers get protected — but nobody in Wisconsin is forced to buy closing counsel the way buyers in attorney-closing states are.
- **Injury deadlines are shorter than you think.** Wisconsin gives you three years to sue for personal injury (Wis. Stat. § 893.54) — but a claim against a government body requires a formal notice of claim within **120 days** (§ 893.80). Miss the second deadline and the case is gone regardless of the first.

## The costs on top of the lawyer's fee

Budget for these separately, because none of them appear in the hourly rate:

- **Filing fees:** a Wisconsin circuit court civil filing generally runs about $265 for a large claim (less for small claims, roughly $95), and divorce filings run roughly $185 to $265 depending on the county and whether minor children are involved. Counties add local surcharges, so treat these as planning numbers, not quotes.
- **Retainers:** Wisconsin family and civil firms commonly ask $2,500 to $7,500 upfront as an advance fee deposit, held in trust and drawn down as work is billed; unearned portions are refundable when the engagement ends. Get the replenishment trigger and refund mechanics in writing — the clauses that matter are itemized in our [retainer agreement guide](/blog/retainer-agreement-clauses-to-read-before-signing).
- **Expert and process costs:** medical experts in injury cases, custody evaluators in family cases, appraisers in farm and business divorces, process servers and court reporters everywhere. Ask whether your agreement bills these at cost or with a markup.
- **Custody add-ons:** when parents cannot agree on placement, Wisconsin courts appoint a guardian ad litem to represent the child's interests, and the parents usually share that lawyer's fee — from several hundred dollars to several thousand, depending on the county and the length of the case. Many counties also require or strongly encourage mediation before a contested custody hearing; private mediators commonly charge $150 to $300 an hour, usually split between the parties. Neither cost appears in either parent's own attorney quote.
- **OWI surcharges:** on a first offense, the $535 driver improvement surcharge, alcohol assessment, and any ignition interlock costs together exceed the base forfeiture several times over — the lawyer's fee is only one line of the real total.

## If these rates are out of reach: Wisconsin's low-cost routes

Wisconsin has a working low-cost legal infrastructure, and it is underused because people assume they earn too much to qualify. **Legal Action of Wisconsin** provides free civil legal aid across the southern half of the state, and **Wisconsin Judicare** covers the northern counties. The **State Bar of Wisconsin's Lawyer Referral and Information Service** offers an initial consultation at a fixed modest rate, and its **Modest Means Panel** matches middle-income clients with lawyers who have agreed to reduced fees — the right purchase when your question is "do I even have a case?" Every circuit court clerk's office can point you to the state's self-help forms and the Wisconsin Court System's online guidance for small claims, divorce, and eviction matters. None of these routes replaces retained counsel in a contested case — but for the large middle of legal problems, they are the difference between Wisconsin's published rates and what a prepared resident actually pays.

## Wisconsin lawyer cost: frequently asked questions

**How much does a lawyer cost per hour in Wisconsin?**
Most Wisconsin lawyers bill $175 to $400 per hour in 2026. Clio's billing data puts the statewide average at $287 per hour — against a $362 national average — with Milwaukee and Madison at the top of the range and smaller-city and rural practices at the bottom.

**How much does a lawyer cost for a first-offense OWI in Wisconsin?**
Defense typically runs $1,500 to $3,500 as a flat fee. The charge itself is a civil forfeiture of $150 to $300 plus a $535 surcharge and court costs, with a 6-to-9-month license revocation — but it counts as a prior offense forever, which is why most people defend it rather than pay it.

**How much does a divorce lawyer cost in Wisconsin?**
An uncontested divorce with a full agreement typically costs $1,000 to $2,500 in attorney fees plus the county filing fee (roughly $185 to $265). Contested cases billed hourly at $200 to $375 run $6,000 to $25,000, with custody disputes and business or farm valuations at the top.

**What percentage does a Wisconsin workers comp lawyer take?**
By statute, no more than 20% of the amount in dispute that is recovered, and the Department of Workforce Development must approve the fee. If liability is admitted and no hearing is needed, the cap is 10% and no more than $250. If nothing is recovered, you owe no fee for the lawyer's time.

**How much does it cost to form an LLC with a Wisconsin lawyer?**
Expect $500 to $1,500 in legal fees for formation with a custom operating agreement, plus the state filing fee — $130 when Articles of Organization are filed online with the Department of Financial Institutions ($170 by paper) — and a $25 annual report fee thereafter.

**Do Wisconsin lawyers offer free consultations?**
Personal injury and workers comp consultations are almost always free, because those cases run on contingency. Family, criminal, and business lawyers commonly charge $100 to $350 for an initial meeting or credit it against the retainer if you hire the firm. The State Bar's referral service offers a fixed-rate first consultation if you want a neutral starting point.

## How to hire Wisconsin counsel without overpaying

Start with the [Wisconsin attorney directory](/attorneys/wisconsin) and shortlist by practice area and city, then run three quotes — one local, one from Milwaukee or Madison, one from a regional hub — because the spread inside Wisconsin is wide enough to matter. In the first meeting, ask the four questions that expose the real price: Is this flat, hourly, or hybrid? What retainer is required, and is any of it refundable if we settle early? Who besides you will bill my file? And what does this cost if it goes the long way, not the short way?

If your matter touches another state — a custody case split with [Kansas](/attorneys/kansas), a business with operations in [Maryland](/attorneys/maryland) — ask whether the Wisconsin firm will coordinate with out-of-state counsel or hand you off entirely, because coordination time is billed time.

The through-line of every number in this guide: Wisconsin attorneys are not cheaper because the work is worth less. They are cheaper because Wisconsin overhead is lower, its average rate sits $75 an hour under the national figure, and two of its fee rules — the 20% comp cap and a small claims ceiling about to rise to $15,000 — are written to keep ordinary people out of expensive legal process. Hire with the table above in front of you, and the first quote you get will rarely be the one you should take.
