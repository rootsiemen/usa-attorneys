**How much does a DUI lawyer cost in 2026?** For a first offense, most drivers pay **$1,500 to $5,000** in attorney fees — typically a flat fee. A second offense runs $5,000 to $10,000, and a case that goes to trial or involves an injury can reach $10,000 to $25,000+. The sections below break down the full DUI lawyer cost: the fee itself, how billing works, and the much larger bill a conviction sends you if nobody fights the charge.

Ask anyone who has been through a DUI what hurt most and many won't say the lawyer's bill — they'll say the five years of car-insurance surcharges, the ignition interlock device humming on their dashboard, and the Uber receipts. A [DUI attorney](/dui-attorney) charges for the defense, but the real question is always the same: what does this whole thing cost, with and without a lawyer?

This guide breaks down what DUI lawyers actually charge in 2026, how the billing works, when a trial changes the price, and — most usefully — the honest math comparing the attorney's fee against the cost of a conviction.

## How much does a DUI lawyer cost? The 2026 fee ladder

Most first-offense DUI cases defended by a private attorney cost **$1,500 to $5,000** in total legal fees. Here's the typical fee ladder for 2026:

| Situation | Typical attorney fee |
|---|---|
| First offense, no trial (plea or dismissal) | $1,500 – $3,500 |
| First offense going to trial | $5,000 – $10,000 |
| Second offense | $5,000 – $10,000 |
| Felony DUI / injury involved / trial | $10,000 – $25,000+ |

About 70–80% of first-offense DUI cases never go to trial, so most people land in that first row. The headline numbers look big until you compare them with what the state charges you — more on that below.

**How published fee data compares.** The ranges above line up with what DUI-specific publishers report: 1-800-DUI-LAWS puts a first-offense flat fee at **$1,000 to $3,000**, and LegalClarity's fee data puts a first offense resolved by plea at **$1,500 to $5,000**, with trial pushing the total to **$5,000 to $10,000 and beyond**. LegalClarity also prices the full cost of a conviction — fines, insurance, programs, and lost privileges — at **$10,000 to $30,000 all-in**, which is the number the attorney fee should always be compared against.

**A state example: Texas.** Texas calls it DWI, and published Texas firm data (McConathy Law) puts the cost of *fighting* a DWI at **$2,500 to $15,000** depending on the county, your record, and whether the case goes to trial — wider than the national band because Texas prosecutors in some counties rarely offer reductions. Your state will have its own band; the pattern (first offense cheapest, trial the multiplier) holds everywhere. ## Flat fee vs. hourly: how DUI lawyers bill

Unlike many lawyers who bill by the hour, most DUI attorneys charge a **flat fee** for the case. That fee typically covers the arraignment, pre-trial hearings, plea negotiations, and the DMV administrative hearing (the license-suspension fight that runs parallel to the criminal case).

Why flat fees dominate DUI work:

- **Predictability.** You know the cost on day one instead of watching the clock.
- **Scope alignment.** The lawyer doesn't earn more by dragging the case out — their incentive is a fast, favorable result.
- **Market practice.** DUI defense is high-volume and repeatable, so firms package it.

Hourly billing shows up mainly for complicated cases — felony DUIs, accidents with injuries, or cases headed for a full jury trial. Hourly rates for DUI defense typically run **$200 to $500 per hour** depending on the market, and a trial can burn 20–60 hours of attorney time. That's exactly why trial fees jump so much.

The catch to watch: some "flat fees" exclude the trial. A quote of "$2,500 flat fee" might cover everything *except* a trial, which then costs another $5,000+. Always ask what the flat fee includes before you sign — the first question on this list should be free of charge.

What flat fees most often **exclude** — the items to ask about by name:

- **Trial** (covered above — the biggest single exclusion)
- **Suppression motions and evidentiary hearings** beyond the standard pre-trial motions
- **Expert witnesses** — toxicology or breath-test experts bill $1,000–$3,500 each and are rarely inside a flat fee
- **Appeals** — a conviction appeal is a separate engagement
- **The DMV/license hearing** in the minority of firms that bill it separately

None of these exclusions is improper. All of them should be in the written fee agreement, in plain language, before you pay anything.

## The hidden bill: what a DUI actually costs without a lawyer

The attorney's fee is only one line item. The state and the insurance companies have their own invoice, and it arrives whether or not you hire a lawyer. Add up a typical first-offense DUI in most states and the "government bill" looks like this:

| Expense | Typical amount |
|---|---|
| Court fines and penalties | $500 – $2,000 |
| Court costs and assessments | $300 – $600 |
| License reinstatement fee | $100 – $500 |
| DUI education / traffic school | $200 – $500 |
| Ignition interlock device (6–12 months) | $800 – $2,500 |
| SR-22 insurance filing + 3–5 years of higher premiums | $3,000 – $15,000+ |
| Towing and impound fees | $150 – $500 |
| **Total, excluding lost income** | **$5,050 – $21,600+** |

The biggest number by far is insurance. A DUI conviction typically raises auto insurance premiums by **80–120% for 3 to 5 years**. If your baseline premium is $1,800/year, a 90% surcharge means an extra $1,620 per year — about $8,100 over five years. In high-cost states, drivers pay five figures in extra premiums alone. An ignition interlock device adds installation (~$100–$200) plus $60–$120 per month for the monitoring lease.

This is the number most people underestimate. The fine feels like the punishment; the insurance is the actual cost.

## The real comparison: attorney fee vs. conviction cost

Here's where the math gets interesting. A DUI attorney is expensive, but so is a conviction. Run two scenarios on a first-offense case:

**Scenario A: You hire a DUI attorney for $3,000.**

The attorney negotiates a reduced charge — say, reckless driving ("wet reckless") instead of a full DUI, or gets the case dismissed on a procedural issue. Many states impose lighter penalties for reckless driving: smaller fine, shorter or no license suspension, no ignition interlock, and a dramatically smaller insurance hit — sometimes 20–40% instead of 90%+.

Estimated total: $3,000 (attorney) + $500 (fine) + $1,000 (traffic school, fees) + $2,500 (insurance surcharge) = **~$7,000**.

**Scenario B: You plead guilty with a public defender or on your own.**

Fines, court costs, a 6-month interlock lease, and a 90% insurance surcharge for 5 years.

Estimated total: $1,500 (fines/fees) + $1,500 (interlock) + $8,100 (insurance) = **~$11,100** — with no professional fighting the charge at all.

The lawyer doesn't need to win a dismissal to earn their fee. Reducing the charge from DUI to reckless driving can save $4,000+ in insurance costs alone, which is more than the retainer. That's not a guarantee — every case is different — but it shows why "saving money by skipping the lawyer" is often the most expensive choice on the table.

One more comparison: [criminal defense attorneys](/criminal-defense-attorney) handling general misdemeanors charge similar flat fees ($1,500–$5,000), but DUI specialists often resolve cases faster because they know the local prosecutors, the standard plea offers, and the exact suppression arguments that work at your county's courthouse. A generalist can do the job, but a specialist's familiarity with the local system is what you're paying for.

## What makes one DUI case cost $2,000 and another $15,000

Five factors move the fee more than anything else:

1. **Offense number.** First offense is the cheapest tier. A second DUI within the lookback period (usually 5–10 years) roughly doubles the fee. Third and felony DUIs move into trial-level pricing.
2. **Blood alcohol level.** A borderline 0.08 case and a 0.20+ case are different universes of work. High BAC often brings mandatory minimum penalties that make negotiation harder and trial prep longer.
3. **Accident or injury.** Property damage or injuries turn a DUI into a potential felony and add a possible civil lawsuit on top of the criminal case.
4. **Trial.** Trials are the single biggest cost driver — a jury trial can add $5,000–$15,000 on top of the pre-trial fee.
5. **Location.** Big-city DUI specialists charge more than small-town general practitioners. County matters too: some counties' prosecutors offer standard reduced pleas freely; others fight everything, and the attorney bills accordingly.

Also note the DMV hearing. Most states run a **separate administrative hearing** for your license suspension, with a tight deadline (often 7–30 days after arrest). Many flat-fee quotes include this; some charge extra for it. Losing the DMV hearing means months of lost driving privileges regardless of what happens in criminal court — so confirm it's covered.

## Questions that save you money before you sign

Five questions to ask any DUI attorney at the consultation (most offer free consultations — take advantage):

- **"Is the fee flat, and does it cover a trial?"** The most important question. Get the answer in writing.
- **"How many DUI cases have you handled in this county?"** Local experience beats generic experience.
- **"What plea options does this county's prosecutor typically offer for a case like mine?"** This tells you whether the lawyer knows the local playbook.
- **"Do you handle the DMV administrative hearing, and is it included?"** See above — a separate $1,000 surprise is no fun.
- **"What's your realistic best case and worst case?"** You want an honest range, not a guarantee. Any lawyer who promises a specific outcome at the consultation is selling you something.

A fair price for a first-offense flat fee in most markets is $2,000–$4,000. Below $1,500, ask what corners are being cut. Above $6,000 for a routine first offense, ask what you're getting that the $3,000 lawyer doesn't offer.

## How people actually pay for DUI defense

Most people don't have $3,000 lying around, and DUI attorneys know it. Common payment structures:

- **Retainer + installments.** You pay a portion up front and the rest in monthly payments. Standard for first-offense cases.
- **Credit cards.** Nearly all DUI firms accept them; the surcharge is cheaper than the insurance surcharge you'll pay without a defense.
- **Flat fee with milestones.** Some firms split the fee: arraignment and pre-trial work first, a separate fee only if the case goes to trial. This keeps the upfront cost down when trial is unlikely.

What about a public defender? If you genuinely can't afford a private attorney, public defenders are competent — but they carry heavy caseloads, can't take every case to trial, and are typically assigned at arraignment, weeks after the arrest when the DMV hearing deadline may already be passing. The cheapest private DUI consultation is free; take at least one before deciding.

## What a second or third DUI costs

Repeat offenses are where both the fee and the state's bill jump a tier. A **second DUI** within the lookback period (5–10 years in most states) typically costs **$5,000 to $10,000** in attorney fees, because mandatory minimum jail time, longer license revocations, and stricter plea policies make every step contested. A **third or felony DUI** usually starts at **$10,000** and goes up from there — felony cases involve grand-jury or preliminary-hearing stages, expert witnesses for toxicology challenges ($1,000–$3,500 each), and a realistic trial, which is where hourly billing at $250–$500/hour replaces flat fees.

The non-attorney side escalates just as fast. Second-offense fines commonly run $1,000–$5,000, ignition interlock periods stretch to 1–3 years, and some states add vehicle forfeiture or mandatory inpatient treatment at your expense. If your first DUI taught you the insurance math, a second one multiplies it: expect SR-22 requirements of 3+ years and premium surcharges that can exceed $20,000 over the full period. This is why repeat-offense cases are the ones where defendants most often say the lawyer was the cheapest part of the outcome.

## The total cost of a DUI: everything added up

Put the attorney fee and the state's bill side by side for a typical first offense:

| Cost item | Plead guilty, no lawyer | Hire a DUI lawyer (reduced charge) |
|---|---|---|
| Attorney fee | $0 | $2,500 – $4,000 |
| Fines & court costs | $800 – $2,600 | $400 – $1,200 |
| Ignition interlock (6–12 mo.) | $800 – $2,500 | Often avoided or shortened |
| DUI school / classes | $200 – $500 | $200 – $500 |
| Extra insurance (3–5 yrs) | $3,000 – $15,000+ | $1,000 – $4,000 |
| **Estimated total** | **$4,800 – $20,600+** | **$4,100 – $9,700** |

The lawyer's fee is the only line you can shop for. Everything below it is set by the court, the DMV, and your insurer — and a good defense outcome shrinks most of those lines at once.

## DUI lawyer cost: frequently asked questions

**How much does a DUI lawyer cost for a first offense?**
Most first-offense DUI cases cost $1,500 to $5,000 in attorney fees, usually as a flat fee covering pre-trial work and the DMV hearing. Cases that go to trial cost more — typically $5,000 to $10,000 total.

**How much does a DUI lawyer cost for a second offense?**
Expect $5,000 to $10,000. Repeat offenses carry mandatory penalties and longer license revocations, so there is more to fight and fewer standard plea offers.

**Is a DUI lawyer worth the cost?**
For most drivers, yes. A reduced charge (such as reckless driving) can cut the insurance surcharge alone by $4,000 or more over five years — often more than the entire attorney fee — and can save your license and your job.

**Do DUI lawyers charge flat fees or hourly?**
Most charge a flat fee for first-offense cases. Hourly billing ($200–$500/hour) appears mainly in felony, injury, or trial cases. Always ask whether the flat fee includes a trial and the DMV hearing before you sign.

**Can I pay a DUI lawyer in installments?**
Usually. Most DUI firms accept a partial retainer up front with monthly installments, and nearly all take credit cards. Many offer a free first consultation — if cost is the obstacle, ask about the payment structure before you decide to go without a lawyer.

**What does a DUI cost in total, including fines and insurance?**
A first-offense DUI conviction typically costs $5,000 to $20,000+ over 3–5 years once fines, court costs, ignition interlock, DUI school, and higher insurance premiums are added — usually several times the attorney's fee.

## The bottom line

A first-offense DUI defense costs $1,500–$5,000 in legal fees, but the *conviction* costs $5,000–$20,000+ in fines, interlock, and insurance over the years that follow. The attorney's fee is the one line item you can negotiate on day one — and it's the one most likely to shrink the rest of the bill.
